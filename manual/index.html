Edition 01 · Working pages

Frameworks that survive contact with reality

Five practical leadership frameworks for decisions, briefs, debriefs, risk, and organizational learning.

In this edition

FM–01 · Decision speed

The two-way-door check

Before escalating, ask whether the decision can be reversed without lasting harm. If yes, give one owner a boundary, a clock, and permission to move. If no, slow down long enough to name the irreversible consequence and the evidence threshold.

Use it
  1. Name what becomes hard to undo.
  2. Set the smallest safe trial.
  3. Choose the review point before acting.

FM–02 · Team alignment

The 90-second brief

A useful brief makes judgment portable. State the situation, the desired outcome, the non-negotiable boundaries, who owns the call, and when the team checks back. Leave room for questions; do not bury uncertainty.

Template

Situation: What changed? Intent: What must be true next? Boundaries: What may not be traded away? Owner: Who decides? Check: When do we regroup?

FM–03 · Learning

The clean debrief

Reconstruct what was known, what was chosen, and what surprised the team. Judge the process with the information available at the time—not with hindsight. End with one named change and one owner.

Prompts
  • What did we expect?
  • What actually happened?
  • Where did the signal arrive?
  • What one practice changes now?

FM–04 · Risk

The red-team pause

For high-consequence calls, assign one person to argue the strongest credible failure case. Give the dissent a timebox and demand evidence. The job is to expose assumptions—not win a debate or delay forever.

FM–05 · Institutional memory

The four-line decision log

Record the date, decision, reason, and review trigger. A short honest record beats a perfect post-hoc story. The log is not surveillance; it is a map of how the organization learned.
