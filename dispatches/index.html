Dispatch 001 · 6 minute read

When the weather edits the plan

Intent keeps a team useful after the original sequence stops making sense.

The first plan is a theory about conditions. When conditions change, repeating the sequence is not discipline; it is attachment. The leader's job is to preserve the outcome and the boundaries while giving the team room to choose a new route.

Say what changed

Do not disguise a correction as if it were always the plan. Name the new fact, what it invalidates, and what still matters. Calm clarity travels farther than confidence theater.

Keep the next move small

When visibility drops, shorten the decision horizon. Pick the next safe checkpoint, assign an owner, and define what evidence will reopen the plan.

The route can change without changing the promise.

Dispatch 002 · 4 minute read

Protect a quiet channel

Teams in motion need one place where facts can arrive without performance. Make it acceptable to report uncertainty, weak signals, and bad news early. Then reward the behavior by responding to the signal—not punishing the messenger.

Dispatch 003 · 5 minute read

Debrief before the story hardens

Memory becomes narrative fast. Capture observations while they are still specific: timestamps, decisions, constraints, and surprises. Separate those facts from interpretation, then decide what practice changes.
